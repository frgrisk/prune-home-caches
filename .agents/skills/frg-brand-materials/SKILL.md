# FRG Brand Materials

Create professional, on-brand materials for FRG (Financial Risk Group). Covers sales decks, proposals, marketing copy, and internal documents.

## When to use this skill

Any time you're producing content that represents FRG externally or internally: presentations, documents, web copy, emails, social posts, proposals, reports, one-pagers, or anything that should look and sound like FRG.

## Quick reference

Before creating any material, internalize these essentials:

### Brand identity

- **Company**: FRG | Financial Risk Group
- **Brand idea**: "Analyze Risk Differently"
- **Taglines**: "Analyze risk differently." / "Build. Run. Illuminate."
- **Category**: Risk Intelligence
- **Website**: frgrisk.com
- **Core purpose**: Creating risk solutions that empower modern financial institutions to make better decisions

### Brand personality

FRG has two personality archetypes that should inform all content:

**Pioneer** (primary): Groundbreaker, innovative, driven, inventive. FRG leads the way. It doesn't follow industry norms. Content should feel forward-looking and confident without being arrogant.

**Maverick** (secondary): Independent, unconventional, tenacious, fearless. FRG challenges the status quo. Content can be bold and direct. Old tools can't keep up, and FRG isn't afraid to say so.

### Core values

Knowledge | Vision | Integrity | Creativity

## Writing in FRG's voice

### Tone

- **Confident, not arrogant**: State FRG's differentiation directly. "Only FRG..." is acceptable. Avoid superlatives without substance.
- **Forward-looking**: Emphasize what's changing in the industry and how FRG leads that change. "Old tools can't keep up" is a recurring theme.
- **Clear and direct**: Financial risk is complex. FRG's communication shouldn't be. Short sentences. Active voice. No jargon for jargon's sake.
- **Empowering**: FRG empowers people at financial institutions. The human is the hero, FRG is the enabler. "Allowing them to make smarter decisions, faster."
- **Human-sounding**: Avoid patterns that read as AI-generated. Vary sentence structure naturally. See the "Don't" list below for specific patterns to avoid.

### Do

- Use "risk intelligence" (FRG's category term) rather than generic "risk management software"
- Emphasize the cloud-native, SaaS delivery model as a differentiator
- Reference real-time insights and speed of decision-making
- Use the lime green color for emphasis on key phrases in visual materials
- Pair serif headings (Chronicle/Libre Baskerville) with sans-serif body (Akkurat/IBM Plex Sans)
- Use short, punchy sentences for headlines: "See Truth in Data." / "Built For You."

### Don't

- Don't use em dashes (the "—" character) anywhere in FRG content. They are a telltale sign of AI-generated writing. Use periods, commas, colons, or break into separate sentences instead.
- Don't use colors outside the approved palette
- Don't describe FRG as just a "software company." It's a risk intelligence platform AND service.
- Don't use clip art or generic stock imagery. FRG has a specific photography system (see below).
- Don't write in an overly formal, stiff corporate tone. Be direct and human.
- Don't make unsubstantiated claims about market position without specific data points
- Don't invent statistics or client counts. If you don't have a real number, use a placeholder like "[XX]+" or ask the user to provide the number. Never fabricate figures like "100+ clients" unless the user has confirmed that number.
- Don't put internal document type labels (like "Sales Leave-Behind", "One-Pager", "Fact Sheet") on the document itself. These are internal terms. The recipient shouldn't see them.

## Creating materials

This skill handles brand guidelines, messaging, and visual identity. For actual file generation, it relies on the `/pptx` skill (presentations) and `/docx` skill (documents). If those skills aren't installed, you can still generate files programmatically using python-pptx, python-docx, or similar libraries, applying the brand rules from this skill.

### Presentations (.pptx)

Use the `/pptx` skill for the actual file creation.

**Note**: Programmatically generated presentations may need minor manual adjustments to text positioning and image placement in PowerPoint. The skill ensures correct branding, colors, messaging, and typography. If FRG PowerPoint templates are available locally (see "Extended assets" below), using one as a base can save time on layout.

Apply FRG branding:

1. **Color scheme**: Black backgrounds with white text for title/section slides. White backgrounds with black text for content slides. Lime (#93C30B) for accent text, highlights, and CTAs.
2. **Typography**: Use Libre Baskerville (or Georgia) for headings. Use IBM Plex Sans (or Arial) for body text and labels. Labels in ALL CAPS sans-serif.
3. **Layout patterns** from the style guide:
   - Title slides: Large serif heading left-aligned, city skyline photo right side
   - Content slides: Heading + subhead left, key statistics (in lime green) right
   - Use large-number callouts for impact stats (lime green, large serif font). Only use real numbers the user has provided. If no real numbers are available, use qualitative callouts like "Cloud-Native", "Real-Time", "On Demand" instead of making up figures.
   - CTA buttons: Black background, white text, rectangular
4. **Photography**: Use brand photos from the assets directory when possible (see "Asset locations")
5. **Preventing text overflow (critical)**: Text overflowing its container is the single most common and damaging layout problem. Every slide you create must be checked against these constraints:
   - **Font sizes**: Body text 16-18pt. Headings 28-36pt. Never go below 14pt.
   - **Bullet limits**: Maximum 4 bullets per content area. Each bullet is one short line (under 60 characters), never a paragraph.
   - **Text box sizing**: On a 13.33" x 7.5" slide, a full-width text box should be at most 11" wide and 4.5" tall. Leave 1" from top (for heading) and 0.75" from bottom.
   - **Column/grid math**: Before laying out a grid, calculate the available space. For a 3x2 grid in an 11" x 4.5" content area, each cell is about 3.4" x 2" after gaps. That fits a 2-4 word title and ONE line of description (under 50 characters). If your description is longer, rewrite it shorter.
   - **The overlap test**: After placing every text element, verify that no two text boxes overlap vertically. Add up: heading top position + heading height + gap + body top position + body height. If that sum exceeds the slide height minus bottom margin, you have overflow. Reduce content or split to another slide.
   - **When in doubt, cut content.** A clean slide with 3 points is better than a cramped slide with 6.
6. **Logo insertion**: When embedding FRG or VOR logos, you must preserve the original aspect ratio. The practical way to do this: read the image file to get its native width and height in pixels, then set the target width (e.g. 2 inches) and calculate height as `target_width * (native_height / native_width)`. Do not set both width and height independently. In OOXML, set `noChangeAspect="1"` on the cNvPicPr element AND compute the correct EMU dimensions from the source image ratio. A distorted logo is worse than no logo.

A standard FRG PowerPoint template is bundled at `assets/templates/frg-template-16x9.pptx`. Use it as a base when possible to get pre-designed slide layouts with proper spacing. Additional templates (VOR-branded, services-focused) are available on SharePoint if needed.

### Documents (.docx)

Use the `/docx` skill for the actual file creation. Apply FRG branding:

1. **Header**: Keep it simple. One logo only: the FRG logotype (full lockup) OR the relevant sub-brand logo, not both. Don't stack multiple logo layers (FRG + VOR + product name). Put the website (frgrisk.com) in the footer, not floating in the header.
2. **Typography**: Chronicle Text / Libre Baskerville for headings, Akkurat / IBM Plex Sans for body
3. **Color usage**: Primarily black text on white. Lime green for section labels and highlighted text. Thin lime/green rule lines as dividers.
4. **Layout**: Clean, generous white space. Two-column layouts for data-heavy pages.
5. **Page limits**: When the user asks for a "one-pager", the output must be exactly one page. Not half a page, not two pages. The content should fill the page well, using the full available space with a balanced layout. Use 10-11pt body text, 0.5-0.75" margins, and structure content to naturally fill the page (header, hero section, key features, benefits, CTA, footer). If content overflows, tighten wording rather than removing entire sections. If content is too sparse, add more detail or use slightly larger spacing. The goal is a professional, complete-looking page.

FRG Word templates are bundled:

- `assets/templates/frg-word-template.docx`: General purpose
- `assets/templates/frg-report-template.docx`: For client reports

Additional templates (technical design, requirements, business plan) are available on SharePoint.

### Sales materials

For proposals, pitch decks, and one-pagers:

- Lead with the problem ("Old tools can't keep up") before the solution
- Include key differentiator: only true SaaS risk intelligence platform
- Tailor content to the audience:
  - **Tech Evaluator** (Architect, VP Technology): Emphasize cloud-native architecture, scalability, integration
  - **End User** (Analyst, Risk Manager): Emphasize real-time insights, ease of use, speed
  - **Economic Buyer** (CRO, CFO): Emphasize ROI, efficiency, compliance, risk reduction
- Include impact statistics formatted as large lime-green numbers
- Close with a CTA and contact information

### Marketing content

For website copy, emails, social media, blog posts:

- Open with a bold, short statement that captures the brand idea
- Reference the approved taglines where natural
- Keep paragraphs short (2-3 sentences max for web/email)
- For social: be conversational but authoritative. The Pioneer/Maverick personality should come through.
- For email campaigns: subject lines should be direct and benefit-oriented

### Internal documents

For reports, specs, and technical documents:

- Use the same typography and color system but tone can be more technical
- Include the FRG logo in headers
- Use the appropriate template from the Templates directory

## Photography system

FRG uses a deliberate photography hierarchy based on an "altitude" metaphor:

| Level        | Altitude  | Subject                                       | Use for                                  |
| ------------ | --------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Services     | 30,000 ft | Aerial satellite views of cities              | Service-focused materials                |
| Products     | 30,000 ft | Aerial natural landscapes (streams, terrains) | VOR product materials                    |
| Industry/FRG | 10,000 ft | City skyline architecture                     | FRG corporate/general materials          |
| Lifestyle    | 5,000 ft  | Candid professional people                    | Customer-facing, people-oriented content |

**Individual VOR products**: B&W versions of aerial landscapes with lime green accent strips.

**Photo treatment**: Aerial photos cropped into horizontal strips, stacked below city skyline photos. This reinforces FRG's narrative: products and services as the foundation supporting the industry.
